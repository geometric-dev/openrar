# v1.33.0 Pre-Analysis — Candidate Source and Block-Table Reuse (Gate 0)

Arc: "the m1/m3 ratio gap is a candidate-source gap, not a parse gap." Base:
v1.32.0 (tag `v1.32.0` @ 70f9b2c). Branch `arc/v1.33.0-candidate-source`.

Clean-room basis unchanged: `docs/spec` + `RAR5-FORMAT.md` for the format.
Mechanisms are derived from the published format plus our own measurements. An
external expert report informed the *hypothesis set*; §3 records its provenance
and exactly which of its claims were checked, refuted, or left unverified. No
reference implementation source or disassembly was consulted, per
`ARCHITECTURE.md` §1.2.

---

## 0. Gate 0 statement

**Claim:** the standing ratio gap to WinRAR (m1 17.6%, m3 2.8%, m5 3.0%) is
caused by *which candidates our encoder can express*, not by how it chooses
among them. Three specific deficits are named and each is independently
addressable without changing the parse: (1) we cannot emit a 2-byte match, (2)
we source candidates only from one hash-chain walk per position plus a fixed
four-distance rep scan, and (3) we re-emit all four Huffman tables every block
when the block header already carries a bit that suppresses that.

**Conditional approval requested, in three tiers.** Tier 1 (table reuse, clamp
fix) is independent of every open question and is approved outright. Tier 2
(length-2/3, sparse probes, the lazy ring) is approved as measured experiments
with per-item gates. Tier 3 (any parse) is **not** approved; it is explicitly
out of scope until the Tier 2 measurements land and the T9 premise is settled
(§4.6).

**The wire format is untouched by Tier 1 and Tier 2.** Every token they add is
one the decoder has always decoded. Legality proof remains the 24-stage interop
gate plus cross-engine decode (UnRAR 7.20 / WinRAR 7.20). The m3
non-regression bound and the v1.31-regression bound (zeros artifact ≤ 3,514 B,
m5 speed lead) remain standing.

### 1.3 What the parked arc measured, and the isolation that reframed it

`arc/v1.32.0-optimal-parse-parked` (`04408fb`) built a rolling forward DP over
`{literal, match, rep0..3, 257}`, priced from the previous block's real Huffman
code lengths. Measured at m3: code −1.95%, text −1.39%, random tie, runs/zeros
+28%, time ~3.9x against a 1.42x budget.

**The un-isolated `compress_tests` failure is now isolated**, and it changes
what those numbers mean:

- It is T9's pure-addition assertion (`tests/unit/compress_tests.cpp`,
  "suppressed-257 output drifted from v1.30.4 reference bytes").
- It fires on **zeros1m at m3 only**. text64k, rand256k and mixed match
  byte-exactly at both m1 and m3.
- **All eight cases decode byte-exact**, including the drifted one. The DP output
  is valid RAR5; nothing is corrupt.
- Cause: `dp_should_enable()` enables the DP for m3 unconditionally
  (`compressor50.cpp:1797-1802` on that branch — `{false,false,false,true,…}`)
  and the 257-suppression test helper does not disable it, so the DP rewrites
  token choice inside a path whose invariant is "suppressing 257 reproduces
  pre-M1 bytes exactly".

Two consequences, both load-bearing:

1. **T9's premise is structurally incompatible with any m3 token-selection
   change.** The gate exists to prove 257 was a *pure addition*; any parse change
   at m3 voids that proof by construction. This is a test-design decision to
   settle before Tier 3, not a hash to refresh — and it is why the parked
   branch's claim of being "a CORRECT engine with an honest measurement" is not
   supportable.
2. **The single drifting corpus is the run-heavy one, with 257 suppressed.**
   That is the runs-pricing failure appearing directly in a controlled A/B
   rather than being inferred. And on the other three corpora the DP reproduced
   greedy's choices byte-for-byte — so the −1.95% / −1.39% wins did not come
   from these inputs at all. The parked branch's headline numbers should not be
   cited as evidence for a parse until re-derived.

Arithmetic that supports the same conclusion independently: 8 MiB of zeros at the
512 KiB block quantum is 16 blocks at ~26 B of table description = 422 B, which is
exactly the number measured. The token payload rounds to zero, so that gate was
measuring table overhead, not parse quality.

## 2. The central question — and why it is open

**Can an encoder that never emits a 2-byte match, never probes a short context,
and re-tabulates every block be within 2.8% of WinRAR by better parse
selection — or is the gap in the candidate set itself?**

It is open because both readings survive the existing evidence. The descoped
arc's own conclusion was that the gap is "structural in the finder"; that
conclusion was drawn from a branch whose ratio numbers §1.3 has just shown to be
unreproducible from its own test corpora. The finder-side reading is therefore
*unfalsified* rather than *established*, and Tier 2 is designed to falsify it
cheaply.

The decisive cheap test is item 6: count length-2 and length-3 tokens in a
reference m1 stream against its m3 stream. If m1 carries them and m3 does not,
the two methods are structurally different parsers and the m1 gap is a parse gap
of a kind we have not built. Ten minutes; it gates a six-week arc.

## 3. Provenance of the external input, and what we could not verify

An independent RAR5 expert was briefed with a self-contained description of the
format, our encoder, and our measurements, and returned a verdict. Recorded
honestly because two of its claims did not survive checking against our tree:

**Refuted against our tree:**

- *"The decompiled RAR 7.23 encoder in your working tree contains both parsers."*
  No decompilation exists in this repository or any of its worktrees; the only
  sources are our own clean-room code. `ARCHITECTURE.md:12` forbids
  reverse-engineered source trees and `docs/spec/00-overview.md:3` is written so
  the format can be implemented without the reference implementation. The advice
  to read the mechanism directly is therefore both factually wrong and outside
  our provenance standard.
- *"Your literal is LD symbol + 8 bits."* `docs/spec/03-compression-m1-m5.md:105`
  already specifies a literal as `LD` Huffman only, 0–255. The error was in our
  briefing, not in the spec or the code. The *economics* point survives and is
  valuable — two short symbols can beat one literal symbol, and a length-only
  arbiter never compares symbol counts (§1.2) — but it is not a defect.
- *"Q8: your LDD predicate is wrong."* Our emitter is already correct
  (§1.2). This was a confirmation, not a fix.

**Accepted as a hypothesis, not as specification.** The remaining mechanism
descriptions — the ~20-candidate per-position list, three sparse-context probes,
a recency list, the static log2 cost expression, the length→distance ring, the
table-reuse bit economics — are internally consistent and plausible, but they
are second-hand. We are not reading the reference binary. Each is therefore in
this arc an **experiment with a gate**, sized so that being wrong costs days, not
weeks, and so that no Tier 2 item is a precondition for another unless the
measurement says so.

**One claim is explicitly the expert's own least-certain assertion** and gates
Tier 3: that the reference selects its shortest-path parse for the two lowest
methods via a `(method − 1) < 2` test. It is consistent with three independent
observations but is unverified. Item 6 confirms or kills it.

## 4. Candidate designs, argued honestly

### Design A — Block splitting with table reuse (Tier 1, no parse change)

**PROPOSED, first.** The block header already carries a bit that means "reuse the
previous block's tables"; our decoder honours it (`decompressor50.cpp:417`,
`:577`) and our encoder hardcodes it on (`compressor50.cpp:1081`, `:1126`).

- For: independent of the parse, of the finder, and of every open question in
  §2. ~26 B of table description per block becomes 3–5 B of framing whenever the
  tables have not materially moved. 64 MiB of zeros is currently ~128 blocks ×
  ~27 B ≈ 3,514 B, essentially all of it table overhead. Decoder side already
  exists, so the change is encoder-only and its legality is argued by a decoder
  we already pass.
- Against: the win is concentrated on run-heavy and other low-entropy input; on
  mixed data where tables genuinely move every block it may be neutral or
  slightly negative. The reuse predicate is a new heuristic that can be wrong in
  both directions.
- Verdict: highest ratio-per-unit-effort in the arc, and the only item that is
  fully decoupled. Ship first, measured on its own.

### Design B — Length-2 and length-3 match support (Tier 2)

**PROPOSED.** Lower `MIN_MATCH` to 2 and add emission for length slot 0 on both
the LD-match and RD-rep length tables, with the admissibility guard below.

Admissible pairs are exactly `increment(d) + 2 ≤ len ≤ increment(d) + 4097`.
Expressed as distance caps: length 2 → distance ≤ 0x100; length 3 → distance ≤
0x2000; length 4 → distance ≤ 0x40000. **Filter, never clamp** (§5, and it is
the same predicate that fixes the clamp bug in Tier 1).

- For: RAR5 has a token class we cannot express at all. On text and code that
  class is dense, and it is precisely the class a length-only arbiter cannot
  see. Targets the largest single gap in the matrix (m1, 17.6%) with close to
  zero time cost — the candidates already exist in the walk, we are only
  permitted to admit shorter ones.
- Against: increases emission frequency of the two cheapest length slots, so the
  per-block RD table gets busier; the gain is not free in table terms and may
  partly cancel against Design A. Also widens the surface where a wrong
  `(len, dist)` silently decodes to a different length — which is why the
  structural clamp fix is Tier 1 and not deferred to here.
- Verdict: approve as a gated experiment. Gate: m1 size must improve; m3 time
  non-regression; zero new interop failures.

### Design C — Three sparse 1/2/4-byte context probes (Tier 2)

**PROPOSED as an experiment, not as an adoption.** At each position, for the
1-, 2- and 4-byte context, look up the most recent earlier position with that
same context in a direct-indexed table, derive a distance, verify ≤ 4 bytes,
admit as a candidate.

- For: adds candidate diversity that a single chain walk cannot rank, at a cost
  of one table load and a ≤4-byte compare per probe — no chain step. This is the
  mechanism that would explain a candidate-source gap.
- Against: this is the largest claimed win in the entire external report and it
  currently rests **entirely** on a description we cannot audit (§3). Three
  tables is real memory on constrained targets, including the ≤64 MiB window
  cases that already fall back off the tree finder. It may also be subsumed by
  Design B.
- Verdict: gate hard. Ship only if it beats Design B alone on the m1 gap; if
  B already closes most of the gap, C is not worth its memory.

### Design D — Length→distance ring for the lazy step (Tier 2)

**PROPOSED.** Record every admitted candidate at the current position into a
ring keyed by `(length, distance)`. When the lazy step defers one position,
look up the record whose length equals `pending length − 1` and take that
distance, instead of re-entering the finder.

- For: the only item on this list that is expected to improve *both* axes. It
  answers "should I split this match?" from memory rather than by a second walk.
- Against: correctness depends on the ring's key space matching what the lazy
  test actually asks for; a miss must fall back to the walk, so the failure mode
  is a silent return to today's cost rather than a wrong answer. Ring sizing and
  eviction are new state.
- Verdict: approve. Lowest ratio-per-effort here but it buys back the time the
  other items spend, which is what makes them affordable inside m3's 2x.

### Design E — The parse, re-scoped (Tier 3) — NOT APPROVED THIS ARC

The descoped DP failed on architecture, not on bugs: it priced rep edges as rep
edges (state-coupled prices) and priced from the previous block's real code
lengths (a parse↔table fixed point that produces a cold start, stale prices and
an unused-symbol penalty). The external report's alternative is a static cost
function that never reads the tables, with a band-limited length ladder anchored
at each candidate's maximum, no state in the price, and one shallow chain call
per position.

**Not approved.** It is gated on three things that do not exist yet: item 6's
token count, Design B/C/D's measurements, and a settled T9 premise (§1.3).
Approving it now would repeat the descoped arc's actual mistake — committing to
a parse before establishing that the candidate set is what is missing. If item 6
says m1 and m3 are the same kind of parser, the honest answer is that the gap is
in the finder and Tier 3 never opens.

## 5. Failure modes

1. **Silent length corruption (highest severity).** `add_match` clamps the
   encoded base length into `[2, 4097]` while the decoder reconstructs
   `base + increment(d)`. An out-of-range `(len, dist)` therefore decodes to a
   **different length** — a one-byte desync, not a rejection, detected only by a
   CRC at end of file. The decoder validates nothing: no length-range check, and
   an out-of-window distance zero-fills rather than failing. The greedy loop
   guards it; any new candidate path (B, C, E) does not until we make it
   impossible. **Fix in Tier 1**, structurally: make the match API take the base
   length plus the distance and derive the covered length internally, so there is
   no quantity left to clamp. Filter, never clamp. Negative test asserts
   `len == base + increment(dist)` after *every* match emission, not just the
   greedy path's.
2. **Table-reuse predicate wrong in the profitable direction.** Reusing tables
   that have moved degrades ratio and, worse, can be silent. Needs an explicit
   drift measure and a bound, not a timer.
3. **A 257 emitted on a stale state.** Guarded by the shadow contract, but Design
   D's ring introduces a new way for a pending length to disagree with what the
   shadow believes. Any mismatch must fall back to the walk.
4. **Streaming identity broken.** Every item must be byte-identical for
   adversarial `feed()` chunking, and per-block state must survive the solid
   chain.
5. **Short-match admission outrunning the distance increment.** Length 2 at a
   distance > 0x100 is illegal by the same predicate as the clamp. B and C share
   this surface; one guard, one test.

## 7. What this arc deliberately does NOT do

- **No parse.** Tier 3 is out of scope until §4.6's gates are met.
- **No finder replacement.** Chains stay. The tree is not revisited.
- **No format change.** No new token class is invented; length slot 0 is used
  because it is already legal.
- **No MT interaction.** v1.32's MT encoder scaling is shipped and untouched;
  these items are single-threaded-candidate work and must not entangle.
- **No decoder change.** Design A's decode side already exists and is pinned by
  existing tests.

## 8. Challenge invited on

1. **Is `increment(d) + 2 ≤ len ≤ increment(d) + 4097` the complete
   admissibility rule, or is a lower bound missing for the rep path specifically?**
   Rep lengths use a different length alphabet (RD, 44 slots) and we have not
   verified that the same predicate governs it. If it does not, Design B's guard
   is wrong on the rep side and we would not know from our own suite.
2. **Does Design A's win survive on mixed data, or is it a runs-only effect that
   inflates the headline?** A runs-only win is still worth having but is a much
   smaller claim than "the block quantum was mis-chosen", and the ROADMAP entry
   should not overstate it.
3. **Is the ~26 B/block table estimate right for our own table emitter, or is it
   inherited from the external report?** If our emitter emits tables more
   cheaply than assumed, Design A's payoff shrinks proportionally.
4. **Does admitting length-2 matches make the RD table so busy that Design A's
   win is cancelled?** B and A interact; §4 Design B says so, but the
   interaction has not been measured.
5. **Is `MIN_MATCH` load-bearing anywhere we have not looked** — WASM builds,
   the CDC chunker, the parallel compressor's chunk geometry? A global constant
   change with a per-target memory story behind it deserves more than one grep.

## 9. Challenges 1 and 5 resolved against the tree

**Challenge 5 — answered.** `MIN_MATCH` has exactly three uses, all in
`compressor50.cpp`: `:593` (finder result rejection), `:1410` (rep arbitration),
`:1440` (fresh-match emission), defined at `compressor50.hpp:194`. Nothing in
WASM, the CDC chunker, `parallel_compressor.cpp`, or the chunk geometry reads
it. The change is local to the compressor.

**Challenge 1 — answered, and the answer corrects Design B.** The predicate does
**not** govern the rep path, and must not:

- Fresh match: the decoder **adds** the increment back
  (`decompressor50.cpp:867-878`), so the wire carries `base` and the decoder
  reconstructs `base + increment(d)`. `add_match` correctly subtracts it and
  rejects when `length <= inc` (`compressor50.cpp:710-715`); the decision loop
  guards and clamps at `:1446-1449`.
- Rep: the decoder does **not** add any increment — `len = slot_to_length(...)`
  and `last_length_ = len` with no adjustment (`decompressor50.cpp:901-903`).
  `add_rep`'s comment at `:762-763` states this mirror and it is correct.

So rep lengths are stored increment-free on both sides and need no lower-bound
guard. Design B's admissibility guard applies to the **fresh-match path and to
any new distance-producing candidate** (Design C's probes), never to rep tokens.
Adding one there would reject legal rep tokens.

**This also refutes part of the external report.** It claimed the decoder
"validates nothing — no distance-vs-window check (out-of-window distances
zero-fill rather than fail)". Our decoder hard-fails: `if (distance == 0 ||
distance > win_size_) return false`, on both the fresh-match path
(`decompressor50.cpp:882`) and the rep path (`:896`, both credited to report
M6). What survives from that claim is only the **length-range** half: the
decoder reconstructs `base + increment` with no range check, so a clamped base
does silently decode to a different length. Failure mode 1 stands; its severity
is "hard-fail at the CRC" rather than "zero-fill and drift", because our own
decoder catches the distance half.
